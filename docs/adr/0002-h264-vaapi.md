# 0002: Deliver the view as H.264 encoded on the iGPU instead of JPEG encoded on the CPU

Date: 2026-11-09. Status: accepted and implemented; the per-pointer-move crop it describes was superseded by [ADR 0003](0003-phone-pans-an-encoded-window.md).

## Weakest part at the time

While the pointer moves, every captured region is a new image, and each one costs about 22 ms of CPU: Hyprland copies the region into shared memory, phonepad compares it with the previous image and `jpeg-encoder` encodes it (about 5 ms, 70 KB). That is 25–27% of a core at about 12 new images/s. The phone then needs a median 33 ms to decode each JPEG, so the delivery path cannot pass 30 images/s even with a free desktop CPU.

## Forces

- The operator wants CPU use close to zero and at least 30 frames/s, keeping JPEG only as a last resort.
- Hyprland renders on the Intel UHD 630, whose VA-API driver (iHD) encodes H.264 in low-power mode. Low-power H.264 accepts only constant-QP rate control.
- Hyprland's wlr-screencopy offers dmabuf captures only as AR24 with the linear modifier.
- One screencopy takes about 50 ms, so the frame rate comes from keeping several captures in flight.
- `unsafe_code` is forbidden in the workspace; a VA-API or ffmpeg binding needs FFI.

## Evidence

Measured on this desktop, 648×1072 region, 300 recorded frames (half static, half a sliding region):

| Path | Bytes/frame | PSNR | CPU/frame |
|---|---|---|---|
| `jpeg-encoder` q60 | 70 KB | 31.6 dB | 5.0 ms |
| H.264 VA-API low power, QP 24 | 4.7 KB | 36.2 dB | – |
| H.264 VA-API low power, QP 32 | 2.7 KB | 32.7 dB | – |

- Encode latency through ffmpeg, one frame in flight: at most about 6 ms per frame.
- Zero-copy capture and encode (`wl-screenrec`: dmabuf capture, VA-API crop, convert and encode): about 1% of a core at about 18 frames/s, about 0.5 ms of CPU per frame; Hyprland CPU 3.0% against 2.9% without it.
- Pipelined dmabuf screencopy, 3 in flight: 56–62 captures/s, the same as shared memory.
- Pixel 7a, WebCodecs `VideoDecoder`, the recorded stream: 150 of 150 frames decoded, median 15 ms, p95 25 ms. `createImageBitmap` on the same frames as JPEG: median 33 ms.
- The colour conversion must be tagged BT.709 limited range; untagged, decoded PSNR falls to about 30 dB at any QP.

## Move

Capture into dmabuf buffers allocated on the iGPU, crop and convert to NV12 on the GPU, encode H.264 with VA-API in low-power mode, and send access units to the page, which decodes them with WebCodecs. Every captured frame is encoded; the CPU comparison of images is dropped, since an unchanged frame costs a few hundred bytes. A new viewer, a zoom change or a region jump gets an IDR frame.

## As built

- Flow control: each phone may have 4 access units unacknowledged. A phone that falls behind receives no delta until the next keyframe and asks for a keyframe only once it has credit to take it (`phonepad-core::stream`); requests from all phones coalesce into one flag, and a requested keyframe is encoded no sooner than 500 ms after the previous keyframe. Force: a slow phone that kept acking recovered one credit at a time and asked each time, so every phone received about 4.7 keyframes a second (40 in 8.5 s for a healthy client; 17 with the interval). The interval is a policy chosen by the implementing agent, not the operator; a new viewer may wait up to 500 ms for its first picture.
- Encode latency is not the 6 ms measured idle: the conversion runs on the render engine, which is 70–98% busy while the pointer moves over a GPU-heavy terminal, and Hyprland's GL context is created with high priority while VA-API contexts are normal priority. One conversion waits 14–55 ms in that queue; raising the VA context's priority needs `CAP_SYS_NICE` (i915 returns EPERM for priority 1 unprivileged). Conversions are therefore submitted ahead of a dedicated encode thread, up to six in flight; a capture surface stays out of the capture pool while a frame cropped from it is in flight (`Cropped` holds an `Arc` of its source surface).
- Measurements as built: README, Measurements.

## Operator decision

2026-11-09, verbatim: "nice, approve all, commit and let's continue hill climbing". Approves: the H.264 VA-API path; a new dependency for VA-API encoding (`cros-codecs`/`cros-libva` or `ffmpeg-next`); exempting one small crate from `forbid(unsafe_code)` if the chosen binding needs FFI calls; the wire protocol and `pad.html` change. Nothing else.

## Preserved

- The auth model, input injection, pointer size and wallpaper behaviour.
- The core state machines: per-session flow control still bounds unacknowledged frames.

## What would reverse it

The full path measuring worse than the evidence above: more than about 5 ms of CPU per frame, encode latency above 17 ms, Hyprland frame times worse than with shared-memory capture, or text on the phone visibly worse than JPEG. Reverting this ADR's commits restores the JPEG path; the installed phone app reloads the page from the server, so no reinstall is needed.
